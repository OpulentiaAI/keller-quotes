Request-note text can affect the candidate pool independently of manufacturing suitability.
The source-bound ablation in [[Keller retained numerical mechanism experiments]] replaced only boilerplate notes with the frozen specification and recovered historical candidates for a previously unpriced request, while the resulting proposal still missed its target.
The [[Keller Git-pinned experimental reports 68c8201]] also records an intentionally unsafe removal of all notes that improved one observed error but discarded supplied process requirements and worsened another.
Those different outcomes rule out treating note deletion as an established general pricing remedy.
The ablations diagnose retrieval sensitivity in retained retrospective attempts, not current worker instructions or prospective accuracy.
