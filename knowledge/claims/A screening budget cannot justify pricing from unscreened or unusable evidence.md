A screening limit is a resource budget, not permission for an unscreened fallback to enter pricing.
The recorded contract in [[Keller Git-pinned experimental reports 68c8201]] admits only explicitly screened candidates and distinguishes unusable exact rows from usable evidence that can consume the bounded screen.
The matched exact-base experiment in [[Keller retained screening-budget experiment]] changed 228 priced and 22 held cases to 231 priced and 19 held out of the same 250 historical internal-calculation targets, with all-pass moving from 50 to 52.
The three newly priced cases still failed the price criterion; the two new all-passes came from previously priced cases.
Among 228 finite-price pairs, nine prices changed, six APEs improved and three worsened, despite no new criterion loss against that exact base.
The earlier-guard comparison separately retained one inherited price-criterion loss.
Coverage recovery and criterion nonregression therefore did not prove improved prices everywhere, manufacturing equivalence or live completion.
