An increase in total passed criteria does not establish a regression-free workflow.
Among the five original baseline/matched pairs unaffected by the alias invalidity, [[Keller retained analog transfer learning evidence]] preserves a rise from 21 to 41 passed checks out of 50 alongside 24 gains and four losses.
Those retrospective pairs still shared the later prior-outcome exposure finding, and the full original attempted population remained 0/18 all-pass.
The later v2-to-v3 matched five-case transition in [[Keller Git-pinned experimental reports 68c8201]] had three gains and eight losses, while v3-to-v4 recovered eight checks without a new loss.
That recovery did not erase the previous losses or produce an all-pass in either five-scored-case cohort.
The sixth preselected case remained preflight-blocked and ungraded, so its criterion flags were unavailable rather than ten observed failures.
Changed scopes and stochastic workers also limit causal interpretation of the v2 comparison.
