Lower median or mean unit-price error can coexist with losses on other declared diagnostics.
The original policy report in [[Keller Git-pinned experimental reports 68c8201]] preserves unchanged numeric coverage on the 47-case diagnostic holdout, with 46 priced in both versions, but a within-band count falling from 13 to 11 despite improved median and mean APE.
The separate supplemental 500-family set retained 481 priced pairs and increased aggregate nominal underquoting even while typical unit-price error improved.
Those extended-dollar observations describe historical single-line proposals, not realized revenue, margin or customer acceptance.
The already inspected holdout was diagnostic rather than untouched confirmation, and the report's policy predates the later screened-admitted pipeline.
It therefore remained an experimental tradeoff rather than evidence for a universally better global weighting rule.
