Whole-file equality establishes the recovered source bytes, not their applicability to a requested quote or proof of first issuance.
The retained PDF source-integrity audit in [[Keller retained physical source and integrity audits]] checked all 39,975 manifest files with no missing file, size mismatch or SHA-256 mismatch.
Its filesystem mtimes nevertheless differed from the manifest by up to 119 nanoseconds, preserving timestamp representation as a separate observation rather than a content failure.
The chronology findings in [[Keller retained chronology and decision-state learning evidence]] show why byte integrity and metadata agreement cannot independently prove that a price-bearing version was available before a cutoff.
A hash also does not establish customer acceptance, supplier payment, manufacturing equivalence or an authenticated reviewer.
