The historical estimator's confidence is an evidence heuristic, not a calibrated probability of price correctness.
In the reconstructed customer-price population in [[Keller Git-pinned experimental reports 68c8201]], only eight of 37 high-confidence cases passed, within a 50-case replay with no exact-part analogs.
Those requests supplied only part number, description, quantity and customer identity, not a validated geometry or manufacturing-cost model.
The separate policy diagnostics likewise show capped fuzzy similarity competing with exact identifiers.
A high similarity or confidence score therefore did not establish current cost, source applicability or a confirmed outcome for these observations.
